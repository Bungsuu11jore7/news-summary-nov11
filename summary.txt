Wednesday, 11 November 2026 — the day in brief.

UKRAINE: Two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended with Ukraine and Russia agreeing in principle to a large prisoner exchange — 1,000-plus on each side — but no ceasefire or peace deal. Kyiv rejected a US proposal on unoccupied Donbas; envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff head to Moscow next. Away from the table, Ukraine hit Russia's Ust-Luga oil terminal; Russian strikes hit six Ukrainian power substations and a Chornomorsk market, killing three.

IRAN: Marco Rubio said US economic pressure had worked well enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer," while warning force remains possible. The Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after death threats. Brent crude settled at $92.76.

CLIMATE: World leaders gathered in Antalya, Turkey, for COP31, where UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the slow climate response had put the world "on a path to destruction."

UNITED STATES: The House voted 211-204 to send impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, killing the effort. Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat from Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith, and Jim Jordan was elected House Republican leader. Markets rebounded — the Nasdaq rose 1.6% — as CoreWeave jumped 25% and Anthropic filed for a listing seeking a $1.4 trillion valuation; hot inflation data made a December Fed rate rise look near-certain.

ELSEWHERE: Israel swore in a new parliament with no government in sight, and Israeli strikes killed seven people in Gaza. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia. Four million workers joined Spain's first general strike in 14 years over housing. Delhi's air stayed poor after Diwali, and actor Stacy Keach died at 85.
